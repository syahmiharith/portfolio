## The problem

A stock count is only one part of inventory work. Employees need to request items; someone must approve the request; fulfillment must change the available stock without losing the history of what happened.

Inventory Request System brings those steps into an operational interface, with a public demonstration of employee and administrator perspectives.

## My role

I’m developing the product workflow and implementation, including the connection between requests and stock. The repository retains the project’s historical attribution and documents its demo context.

## Decisions that shape the product

### Show availability in context

The inventory table exposes stock, reserved quantities, stock health and active demand together. A number becomes more useful when the person acting on it can understand what is already committed.

### Treat fulfillment as one operation

The request service implements transactional stock deduction. A request’s completion and its inventory effect belong together; partial updates would leave the next operator with conflicting information. The repository includes focused fulfillment integration tests.

### Separate a role demonstration from access control

The public role selector makes employee and administrator journeys inspectable. It is deliberately a demonstration, not proof of production authentication or authorization.

## Current boundaries

This is a portfolio demonstration with simulated roles and seeded data, not a company production deployment. This review did not mutate shared demo stock or repeat the complete approval-to-fulfillment journey. No operational adoption, uptime or concurrency benchmark is claimed.

## Keeping it useful

The complete operational journey is being improved in a separate release. This story will be updated after the deployed request, approval, fulfillment and history paths are verified together. Source changes and test evidence remain inspectable in the repository.
