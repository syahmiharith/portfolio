## The problem

A receipt image is easy to collect and awkward to use. Someone still has to read the merchant, date and amounts, then check the result before it becomes a trustworthy record.

Receipt-to-Form explores that handoff: use extraction to reduce transcription, while keeping the person responsible for reviewing the fields.

## My role

I’m developing and refining the receipt-review experience and its implementation. The repository preserves the project’s origin and attribution; this story describes the current work without claiming sole authorship of its history.

## Decisions that shape the product

### Treat extraction as a draft

The product presents itself as human-reviewed extraction. Editable fields and validation are essential because a plausible-looking amount is not necessarily the amount on the receipt.

### Make input constraints visible

The deployed entry explains accepted image formats and upload limits before a person selects files. A useful boundary belongs at the point where it changes someone’s action.

### Test predictable behavior separately

The repository includes deterministic mocked tests for the review flow. That makes interface and validation regressions easier to reproduce without treating a variable model response as a fixed contract.

## What is verified

On 3 November 2026, the deployed upload entry loaded and showed JPG, PNG and WEBP support, with a five-file and 5 MB per-file limit. The screenshot is a capture of that live entry. Review and testing documentation are available in the repository.

## Current boundaries

This portfolio review did not complete a new live extraction-to-export run. Extraction quality, export completeness and persistence are not asserted here. Do not use the demo as a permanent receipt archive. No accuracy percentage or time-saving claim is published without a measured basis.
