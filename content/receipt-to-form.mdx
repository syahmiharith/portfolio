## Receipts in. Expenses ready.

Expense preparation starts with scattered receipt images and ends with a spreadsheet. I designed Receipt-to-Form to connect those two points: import a batch, correct the extracted details beside the source, and export the values on screen.

## What I designed and built

I own the product from interaction design through full-stack implementation and release. That includes the receipt editor, image inspection, batch processing, browser recovery, spreadsheet output and the tests that protect the workflow as it evolves.

Next.js, React and TypeScript power the application. IndexedDB keeps receipts and corrections on the device; shared Redis limits protect live extraction.

## Design decisions

### Keep the source and the correction together

An expanded receipt shows its fields, extracted items, notes and image in one place. Long merchant and item names grow to fit. Quantities, prices and receipt type use compact controls; currency supports search. Image zoom, rotation and keyboard panning help inspect the source, with an image/form switch on small screens.

Corrections to expense fields and item names, quantities and prices save as they are entered. Valid selected receipts can be exported directly. Validation identifies the fields that need attention, while original extraction confidence stays separate from human edits.

### Make a batch resilient to partial failure

People can upload up to five images or open a ZIP and choose the images inside. Optional samples use the same queue and editor. Two extraction slots process the batch, and each card reflects its actual request state through a compact status and moving bottom border.

A failed receipt can be retried on its own or through Extract. Successful receipts keep their values and corrections. Stop finishes active requests and leaves the rest queued; refresh restores saved work and makes interrupted requests retryable.

### Keep the output connected to the receipt

One Export action offers CSV for spreadsheets and JSON for provenance. CSV contains one row per valid selected receipt, with current expense details and line items. Receipt IDs and source filenames connect each row to its image. JSON also retains the original extraction and confidence.

Dates, currency and totals are validated before export. Spreadsheet text cells are protected against formula execution. Browser-saved images and corrections expire after seven days without an edit and can be deleted from the app.

## What I improved after launch

I refined the product into one consistent workflow: optional samples and uploads share the same editor, corrections save automatically, and export sits in a single batch toolbar. I replaced separate download controls with a compact, keyboard-accessible format menu and kept Extract available while receipts still need attention.

I also addressed practical details revealed through runtime checks: wrapping long names on mobile, removing repeated notes, keeping currency searchable, supporting ZIP selection, and preserving successful work when another extraction fails.

## What I own in production

I maintain the interface, extraction integration, shared request limits, storage recovery and release checks. Regression coverage follows actual tasks: correct a total, refresh, inspect the downloaded CSV, retry a mixed batch and verify that the successful receipt was never sent again. The checks also compare receipt IDs and original confidence across the journey.
