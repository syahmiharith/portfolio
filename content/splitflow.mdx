<h2 id="overview">Agreement before the organizer pays</h2>

An item exclusion changes everyone's share. A claimed transfer still needs checking. SplitFlow puts costs, participant responses and payment claims into one review flow. My work covers the agreement workflow, calculation guards and regression tests.

<h2 id="decisions">Three decisions, in the product</h2>

<Decision number="01" title="A payment claim is a review task" summary="Saying “I paid” cannot make a group ready to settle." screen="splitflow-readiness">

The organizer sees the unresolved claim and can confirm or dispute it. Readiness checks both claimed and disputed records. Participant notes remain distinct from verified money: this prototype does not perform bank verification.

</Decision>

<Decision number="02" title="Calculate the final share in code" summary="A parsed expense is a draft. Typed logic owns the amounts." screen="splitflow-shares">

Item eligibility, exclusions and rounding are explicit. Splitting ₩10,000 three ways must still allocate all ₩10,000, with the remainder assigned consistently. Each participant can inspect the included items, fronted amount and net balance.

</Decision>

<Decision number="03" title="A changed share needs fresh agreement" summary="Accepting an earlier amount cannot approve the new one." screen="splitflow-overview">

When an opt-out changes remaining shares, the workflow requires reconfirmation. Agreement state and settlement readiness stay separate, so a draft, waiting response or unresolved claim remains visible as work to do.

The capture shows the group overview and its agreement states; the calculation and reconfirmation rules are supported by the linked tests.

</Decision>

<h2 id="engineering">Build, maintain, verify</h2>

### A change worth keeping

The readiness check previously looked only for claimed payments. The linked change includes disputed payments too, with regression cases for conflicting claims, rounding, changed shares and non-integer money. Those rules can be inspected in the implementation and tests rather than inferred from a screenshot.

## Current boundaries

The public prototype exposes a seeded group and organizer/participant perspectives. These product captures were taken on 9 November 2026; the public dashboard was rechecked on 8 November. No bank transfer is verified. Durable multi-user operation, authenticated participant journeys and real payment transfers remain outside this case study's verification scope.
