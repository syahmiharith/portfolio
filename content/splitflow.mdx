## The problem

An organizer fronts a group expense, then discovers that someone skipped an item, another person opted out, and a claimed transfer still needs checking. The total is simple; getting everyone to agree on their share is the work.

SplitFlow puts item eligibility, participant responses and payment claims into one review flow.

## My role

Agreement workflow, calculation guards and regression tests. The demo uses local state so visitors can inspect both organizer and participant perspectives.

## Decisions

### Calculate every share in code

Parsing an expense produces a draft. Typed calculation logic assigns item costs, rounds KRW amounts and reconciles the shares with the total. Splitting ₩10,000 three ways must still allocate all ₩10,000, with the remainder assigned consistently.

### Keep disputed money out of readiness

The dashboard makes unresolved claims an action for the organizer. A participant saying “I paid” cannot make the group ready to settle. The readiness logic checks both claimed and disputed records.

### Reconfirm when the agreement changes

When an opt-out changes the remaining shares, the workflow requires reconfirmation. Acceptance of an earlier amount cannot stand in for acceptance of the new one.

## A change worth keeping

The readiness check previously looked only for claimed payments. The linked change includes disputed payments too, and adds regression cases for conflicting claims, rounding, changed shares and non-integer money. These rules can be inspected in the implementation and tests, rather than inferred from the interface.

## Current boundaries

The deployed dashboard was checked on 3 November 2026; the implementation and test changes were reviewed on 4 November. This is a local-state prototype: authenticated identities, durable shared records and real payment transfers are outside the demonstrated scope.
